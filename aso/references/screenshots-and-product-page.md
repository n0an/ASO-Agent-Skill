# Screenshots and the product page

The product page has seconds to prove the app is exactly what the searcher typed. Icon, name and subtitle win the tap; screenshots, or a preview or search results asset when set, win the download; optimize the two rates separately. Asset specifications, what search results show, review rules, test limits and the conversion definition are Apple-documented; what converts and whether screenshot text is read for search are not, so those rules stay observed, and Apple's advice ("Apple recommends") never overrides a tested rule. Indexing and ranking inputs: `references/fundamentals.md`.

## What the page has to do

- Icon, name and subtitle drive impression-to-view; a page that proves the app does what the visitor already believes drives view-to-download. A page-one rank with weak creatives still loses. Nobody reads the description.
- Search results show each app's rating and, by default, its first one to three screenshots (Apple-documented). Budget about 8 seconds (rule of thumb) to answer "is this the app I need?", judged against the whole results page, two ads included since March 2026. Fix visibility first; see `references/keyword-research.md`.
- Guideline 2.3 requires description, screenshots and previews to reflect the core experience and stay current with new versions (Apple-documented). Expectations must hold through the paywall: a treatment can lift downloads while attracting users who never pay.
- The default Apple Ads ad uses the default page's screenshots and previews in upload order, so your first slides are your default search ad; an ad variation is a custom product page (Apple-documented). Ad, page and onboarding show the same thing: Apple Ads visitors tolerate technical screenshots, Meta and TikTok visitors want the ad's promise.
- Never run one generic page; relevance, not price, drives the download. Route intents to custom product pages (up to 70, Apple-documented); see `references/custom-product-pages-and-events.md`.
- Ratings seal the deal and sit beside the screenshots in results (Apple-documented); a low count reads as "not enough people use it" (dated: 2018-2021). See `references/ratings-and-reviews.md`.

## Screenshot sequence and story

- What fills the search result (Apple-documented): a preview always plays first, muted; on iOS 27 and iPadOS 27 a search results image or video replaces the screenshots; one to three screenshots, by orientation, show only when neither is set. Whatever comes first must pass the slot-one test.
- Slot one: what the app is and why, one reason in the biggest type, never a feature list ending in "and more". The caption echoes the first keyword in the name, not the brand. If a built-in app covers the use case, say what it cannot do.
- Keep app UI under the slot-one caption. Guideline 2.3.3: screenshots should show the app in use, not merely title art, a login page or a splash screen; text and image overlays are allowed (Apple-documented). A caption-only opener relies on reviewer latitude; it is an experiment, and brand art now has the header asset.
- Slots one and two form the understanding, three is visible without a swipe, most visitors never reach the last. Put the most-wanted features in the first three, sized for the small results view; slot two gets the category's most-wanted benefit. If a feature deserves slot two, ask whether it deserves the name.
- Lead with what you built the app for, basics second, widgets late; show the result, not the setup; a method-based app opens with its system as a flow. If the app supports Dark Mode, Apple recommends at least one Dark Mode screenshot; put it late.
- Apple allows 1 to 10 screenshots per device size (Apple-documented). Use all 10 (rule of thumb): four or five use cases, then features, then one slide per audience. Not finding five reasons is itself a warning.
- Each caption sets up the next; the default set tells one broad story, niche angles go to custom product pages.
- No onboarding, login or splash screens (Guideline 2.3.3). Caption games too, though games alone can run raw gameplay. A two-slot panorama works only in slots one to three.
- Reorder before redesigning (contested): 2025-2026 practice calls it the highest-leverage change, 2022 practice said it "probably will not change much". Apple recommends even a simple reorder test, and a reorder-only treatment of approved screenshots starts without review (Apple-documented). Follow the newer view: if slots one and two lack keywords and benefits, move later slides up now.

## Captions

- Benefits, not features: benefits won almost every test, one exception in six years (observed). Exceptions: advanced audiences and developer tools where compatibility is the benefit; the reviews tell you which.
- A benefit helps only if people search for it, so pair it with the feature that delivers it. Active captions match searched phrases: "Track Sleep Patterns" yields "track sleep", "sleep patterns" and the full phrase. Drop passive ones such as "Easy to Use".
- Show, don't tell: a benefit with no UI behind it is the most common mistake. Audit every caption for knowledge only the developer has.
- "AI" (contested): early 2025 said use it; from August 2025 it adds little except for chatbots. Follow the newer view: show what the AI does, no model versions.
- One message per screenshot in the largest font, UI cropped to the caption's subject, no sub-captions, pills, emojis or fine print. One keyword theme each: "Fast, Private, and Easy" is three captions. Target 4-5 words (dated: 2020). Apple recommends one main benefit per screenshot after the first.
- No superlatives. "#1" is believable only around 10,000 ratings at 4.9 (rule of thumb). Apple bans unverifiable claims in the subtitle (2.3.7) and creative assets (2.3.14) (Apple-documented). Qualify any "featured".
- Social proof on screenshot one as the biggest element, required now with competition. Thresholds are contested: above about 1,000 ratings at 4.3-4.4 (April 2025) versus 450 five-star ratings or 5,000 already reading as a lot (February and July 2025). It must be true and match the visible rating; fake Apple-style badges are net negatives; one to three short raving reviews beat "1,000+ users" (observed). Apple recommends accolades in the description or promotional text instead; that is advice, so the tested rule stands.
- Keyword reinforcement since mid-2025 (observed; Procedures, item 1): captions reinforce the fields, weigh less and do not compete with them (`references/fundamentals.md`). Repeat count is contested: June 2025 held one mention enough; 2026 practice puts name and subtitle keywords in at least the first three captions, about three times across the set, varied terms beating one repeated. Follow 2026, never at conversion's cost.
- Apple does not document caption reading: its searchable fields are name, subtitle, keywords and company name; tags come from "metadata", which Guideline 2.3 defines to include screenshots. Treat every caption-indexing rule as observed. Since January 2026 the whole image is assumed read; caption position is a conversion test only.
- Legibility serves people and the text reader: maximum contrast, no text over busy UI, fewer words rather than smaller type, no thin, condensed, cursive or layered text, no glow or blurred shadow. Never let a Liquid Glass rendering be a keyword's only appearance.

## Visual rules, specifications and review

- All-marketing and half-and-half sets pass, with no rejection purely for style seen in years (observed), but Apple's 2.3.3 text is stricter: keep the app visible in any slide you cannot afford to have rejected.
- Review rules for screenshots and previews (Apple-documented): no prices, terms or descriptions foreign to the metadata type, which covers "free" (2.3.7); no other mobile platforms' or marketplaces' names or imagery, nothing irrelevant (2.3.10); rights to everything shown and fictional account data (2.3.9); 4+ imagery even when the app is rated higher (2.3.8); state which featured items or subscriptions cost extra (2.3.2).
- Files (Apple-documented, November 2026): .jpeg, .jpg or .png, no alpha or transparency. With identical UI across devices and localizations, upload only the highest required resolution; App Store Connect scales down. iPad apps need an iPad 13-inch set, 2064 x 2752 or 2048 x 2732 portrait.
- The required iPhone class is stated two ways on Apple's page: medium Dynamic Island (1179 x 2556 or 1206 x 2622) versus large Face ID (1284 x 2778 or 1242 x 2688) when large Dynamic Island is absent. Upload large Dynamic Island (up to 1320 x 2868) and medium Dynamic Island sets to satisfy both.
- iPhone Duo screenshots are required from April 2027, for iOS 27.1 SDK apps per the specification page, for every submission per Apple's 2026 What's New; plan for the wider reading. They add a "made for iPhone Duo" badge (Apple-documented).
- Frame raw UI and caption why; uncaptioned sets lost in every observed case (dated: 2021). Real UI when it matches the promise, stylized only when the real one is unpresentable. A current iPhone frame, shrunk and varied; never repeat the icon. Nobody pinches to zoom: show the function or a zoomed-in view.
- Orientation decides whether one or up to three screenshots show in results (Apple-documented; which shows three is not printed). Portrait packs more; landscape usually converts worse, not universally. Baseline on competitors, then test; Apple recommends not carrying a Google Play result over.
- Color: dark versus light is untested, so run the flip as test one. Tint pure black. A plain background fails in a crowded niche. Layout and fonts first, colors next: chubby fonts for friendly consumer apps, thin and tall for premium and money apps.
- People lift conversion in screenshots, real or illustrated; a hand holding a phone counts. Avoid AI-art looks. Previews may not show people holding or tapping a device (Apple-documented).
- Localize imagery with the app; UK buses outconverted US buses in the UK with identical language (observed). A new language inherits the primary language's screenshots, and a language without a preview shows the next best language's (Apple-documented). Apple recommends localizing first where the app is popular and reading low territory conversion as a localization signal. Extra US-indexed localizations need no localized screenshots, though September 2026 practice gives their captions a second keyword focus. See `references/localizations.md`.

## Icon

- The icon owns the tap, screenshots own the download; it shows in search results and across the system (Apple-documented). Its weight is contested: October 2025 called it a bigger lever than screenshots, with banner-like descriptive icons beating minimalist logos; November 2026 called it little leverage.
- Stand out while signaling the category: about 80% of icons are a circle, a letter or a mascot (observed). Category cue in a color no competitor shares; Apple recommends a simple icon without unnecessary detail. Share fonts with the screenshots.
- Guideline 2.3.8: the name and all icons, alternates included, should be similar, all at 4+ (Apple-documented). A test icon that looks like another app risks rejection.
- Test, do not ship (Apple-documented since 2021): alternate icons must be in the live version's binary (asset catalog, Xcode 13 or later, 1024 x 1024); then an icon-only test starts without App Review; a downloader keeps the treatment icon; a winner becomes permanent only as the default icon in the next version, because applying a treatment applies screenshots and previews only. Older guidance said icon tests returned in October 2025; Apple documents no gap. Outside a test, an icon change takes a new version.
- A "clicky" icon drives organic uplift even when Meta traffic lands on the page (observed). In Apple's 2023 case the winning icon was 8% better at over 98% confidence and was applied only after checking downstream performance.

## App preview video

- A preview only moves conversion; Apple leaves it out of the searchable fields. It always appears before screenshots unless its aspect ratio differs (then "A Closer Look"), autoplaying muted on the page, in results and on the Games and Apps tabs (Apple-documented). None beats a bad one; every claim in it must also be a screenshot. In Apple's 2023 case the page without its preview won by 3.3%; Apple recommends testing rather than assuming.
- The niche decides: photo and video editors need one, games profit, single-feature utilities can be hurt. Adding one when competitors have none is contested (February 2025 yes, August 2025 no).
- Content: a captioned tour of the screenshot features, main use case in the first 5 seconds, realistic pace; an overstuffed one wins the download and loses the payment. Apple recommends copy on screen and no seasonal references or prices.
- Rules (Apple-documented, 2.3.4): screen captures of the app only, narration and overlays allowed; all ages; rights in all territories; purchase, subscription or login features disclosed in footage or end frame.
- Specifications (Apple-documented): up to three per localization and device size, 15-30 seconds, 500 MB, 30 fps, H.264 or ProRes 422 (HQ); 886 x 1920 portrait or 1920 x 886 landscape for every current iPhone; processing up to 24 hours.
- The poster frame is an extra screenshot, shown when autoplay is off and during install; it defaults to 5 seconds and locks on approval, so changing it means a new preview submission (Apple-documented).
- Previews have been testable since 2021, up to three per test (Apple-documented); "videos cannot be A/B tested" (February 2025) was wrong. Test video versus none periodically.

## Description, promotional text, name

- The description is not an App Store search field, but it feeds web search results and Apple Ads relevance (Apple-documented). Up to 4,000 characters, plain text; only the first sentence shows without "more"; it changes only with a new version (Apple-documented). Apple recommends no keyword padding and no prices.
- Promotional text: 170 characters above the description, editable any time without a version, settable per custom product page, not a ranking input (Apple-documented). What converts in it is untested.
- Apple recommends a name that hints at what the app does and a subtitle that explains its value; both 30 characters, the subtitle changes only with a new version (Apple-documented). See `references/metadata-writing.md`.

## Header and search results assets

- Announced at WWDC on June 8, 2026 for iOS 27 and iPadOS 27, all categories; open to all developers in App Store Connect from early October 2026. The header is the page's first visual; the search results asset replaces the default screenshots in App Store and Apple Games search. Both are settable per custom product page and per keyword and testable (Apple-documented).
- Apple recommends the header for brand and the search asset for core value; both complement screenshots. Treat the search asset as slot one. Apple does not say whether their text is indexed: do not stuff it.
- Specifications (Apple-documented): header 21:9 at 3840 x 1646; search results 3:2, 1920 x 1280 to 3840 x 2560; both also take 16:9 at 5244 x 2950 .png; no alpha. Videos 5-30 seconds, 30 or 60 fps, looping, muted in results with no unmute. "Use header asset in search results" works only for universal assets.
- Guideline 2.3.14: on-brand, all audiences, no misleading references to other apps, unverifiable claims or prices (Apple-documented).
- Publish with a version, or standalone through Asset Library: once approved, swap onto the live page without a new submission; approve seasonal variants ahead (Apple recommends). Apple Ads use was coming soon in November 2026.
- Render every page in App Store Connect's product page preview before submission: devices, orientations, languages, Dark Mode, iPhone Duo, custom product pages and tests (Apple-documented).
- In-app event cards sit above the screenshots for past downloaders and below for others, and replace the screenshots in search for past downloaders (Apple-documented, 2021-2022). See `references/custom-product-pages-and-events.md`.

## Testing with product page optimization

- Product page optimization is the only A/B platform for the listing: free, about 10 minutes to set up. Keep one running; steps in Procedures.
- Limits (Apple-documented): one test per app at a time; up to three treatments plus the original; 90 days or until stopped; no edits after the start; a stopped test cannot restart, only be recreated. Older guidance reported a 30-day expiry (July 2026); the limit is 90 days. Traffic splits evenly across treatments; a single treatment takes 1-50%, never more than the original. All localizations are selected by default; visitors outside the chosen ones are excluded.
- Eligibility (Apple-documented): live app, Ready for Distribution or Pre-Order Ready for Distribution; Account Holder, Admin, App Manager or Marketing role; treatments show on iOS 15 or later; no tests on custom product pages, Apple Watch or iMessage pages.
- Testable (Apple-documented): icon, screenshots, previews, and since 2026 header and search results assets (in the editor, not yet on the overview pages). Name, subtitle, description and keywords are not, so keyword placement cannot be tested.
- Treatments (Apple-documented): each starts as a copy; unedited elements show the original. Assets need approval but no new version. A visitor sees one treatment everywhere for the whole test.
- Applying a treatment (Apple-documented) is the documented way to change live screenshots and previews without a version: one per test, to the original and to versions in Ready for Distribution or Prepare for Submission; irreversible; it stops a running test.
- Never swap a set in a new version: that cannot separate the change from seasonality. Ship it as a treatment. A release during a test may affect results if it carries tested assets (Apple-documented); schedule tests between releases and watch traffic mix and campaigns (Apple recommends).
- Setup: original plus one challenger (dated: 2021); abrupt variants (August 2025); one asset per test (Apple recommends). Older guidance kept one localization per test; Apple warns fewer localizations slow a test and recommends icon tests across all locales, market-specific assets in their own localization.
- Decision rule (contested): Apple recommends acting only at 90% confidence and ending inconclusive tests; 2026 practice may switch on an even result. Result labels, sizing, revenue and paid traffic: `references/measurement-and-cadence.md`.
- A custom product page in Apple Ads is not a test; compare it against the default page sequentially, two weeks each.
- Cheapest tests first: reorder; benefit set against feature set; caption-only opener; outcome statistic in slot one; review-language lead; a person; video removal; the icon. Apple's menu adds brand-led design, seasonal content, orientation and value proposition; its 2023 evergreen first screenshot beat a seasonal one in December, and Apple calls such results app-specific.
- Ideas come from one- and two-star reviews and competitors' praised features; see `references/competitor-research.md`.
- A live treatment's text is not indexed (observed; Apple silent). Screenshot changes carry no penalty; keep to twice a month (observed; Apple sets no cap, and changes take up to 24 hours to appear).

## Measuring conversion

- App Store Connect's conversion rate is total downloads over unique impressions, every page view also counting as an impression (Apple-documented); tests and peer benchmarks use it. The 40-50% impression-to-view and 3-4% view-to-download rules of thumb are hand-computed page-view ratios; never compare the two. Definitions and benchmarks: `references/measurement-and-cadence.md`.
- Diagnosis: decent impression-to-view with abysmal view-to-download means the name promises the wrong thing, too few screenshots or a keyword mismatch; remove unfocused keywords even where the app ranks. Ranks and impressions up with downloads flat means the page.
- Conversion pays once traffic exists; 50 impressions a day is invisible, not broken. Conversion as a ranking input is observed; Apple names downloads, ratings and reviews, not conversion rate.
- Per-keyword conversion is unknowable organically, and App Store Search figures include Apple Ads (Apple-documented). A one-week low-budget Apple Ads test shows what converts; see `references/apple-ads.md`.

## Procedures

Dated by the year written; the rules above win.

1. Caption rewrite (June 2025): list the name and subtitle keywords; one active caption per screenshot reinforcing one; long-tail variations, checked for demand, later; delete passive phrases; apply the legibility rule.
2. Competitor caption audit (June 2025): search a target keyword; open each top result's screenshots; note caption text, position and relation to the app.
3. Conversion baseline: record App Store Connect's conversion rate and, labeled separately, downloads over unique page views for the last full month (dated: 2021); re-measure both after every change.
4. Product page optimization test (Apple, 2026): Product Page Optimization, Create Test; name it after what it tests; one treatment; traffic; localizations (all unless market-specific); optional duration estimate. In the treatment use the App Icon tab (shown only with alternate icons in the binary) and the Header and Search Results or App Previews and Screenshots tabs. Start Test, Add for Review, Submit. Read it per `references/measurement-and-cadence.md`. Adopt with Apply Treatment to Current Product Page; set a winning icon as default in the next version. Run an idea at least twice (dated: 2022).
5. Header and search assets (Apple, 2026): prepare 21:9 and 3:2 images or 5-30 second videos; upload to Asset Library; submit standalone; check in the preview; on the live version open Header and Search Results, Browse Assets, pick, Publish.
6. Compliance check (Apple): app in use; previews are screen captures; extra purchases disclosed; no prices, other apps or unverifiable claims; 4+ imagery; no other platforms; rights held; required sizes.
7. Timed sequential test, the fallback for what the native test cannot hold (dated: 2020-2021): version A for 14-30 days, version B for the same window, compare, keep the winner, revert fast on a sharp drop.
8. Test menu (dated: 2021): background light versus dark, solid versus pattern, caption color, style, font, capitalization, length, tone, position, size, padding, panorama, order, orientation, humans, social proof. Test plan (Apple, 2022): hypothesis, treatments, traffic, localizations.

## History and changes

Product page optimization and custom product pages (35 then) shipped in December 2021; Apple's test limits are unchanged since January 2022. Dated inline above: October 2025 (twice-a-month cap, caption reading narrowed), November 2025 (70 custom pages), January 2026 (whole-image reading), March 2026 (two ads), June to October 2026 (header assets), April 2027 (iPhone Duo).

## Checks

- First visual (preview, search asset or slot one): what plus one reason, first name keyword, app visible; slot two the top benefit; 10 slots; no onboarding, login or splash screens.
- Captions active, one theme each, backed by UI; name and subtitle keywords in the first three; legible at thumbnail size.
- Review: no prices or "free", other platforms, unbacked "#1"; rights held; fictional data; 4+; extra purchases disclosed; social proof matches the rating.
- Files: no alpha; large and medium Dynamic Island and iPad 13-inch sets; iPhone Duo planned; page checked in the preview.
- Video: screen captures only, no hands on devices, use case in 5 seconds, poster frame chosen.
- A test running between releases, one asset, abrupt variant; set changed at most twice a month.
- Conversion: Apple's rate and page-view ratios recorded and labeled separately.

## Open questions

- Caption indexing mechanics, none documented by Apple: how much is read, position and size weight, repeat weight, iOS 27 changes, header asset text, treatment and custom page text (one observation says not read).
- Contested: icon weight, video when competitors lack one, "AI", social proof thresholds, US-locale captions, Apple Ads traffic in tests (Apple's 2023 talk includes paid referrals; the documentation never names Apple Ads), reorder leverage, switching on an even result.
- Apple-internal gaps: required iPhone class; iPhone Duo scope; header assets on every test path; a release during a test; whether the default ad shows a running treatment; creative assets in Apple Ads.
- Where review draws the 2.3.3 line for caption-only slides. Untested: promotional text, arrows, familiar colors, people for utilities, dark versus light.
