# Competitor research

Competitors are the dataset, not the template: the top five results for your head term encode what Apple rewards, so read their listings, organic keyword lists and growth signals, then find what they leave open. Every download, revenue, demographic and impression-share figure here is a third-party estimate from public store data; Apple publishes no figure for any named app, and its peer benchmarks show anonymized percentiles for your own app only, so they cannot validate an estimate (Apple-documented). Apple documents no competitor-research method: it names search inputs without weights, says to research how similar apps are categorized, and makes "never use a competitor's name" and "never copy" review rules. "Popularity" is Apple's 0-100 search popularity score, floor 5 (`references/keyword-research.md`). "(dated: year)" marks 2019-2024 rules kept where they still explain behavior or give a procedure; where old and new disagree, both are stated.

## Pick the competitors worth studying

- A competitor is any app ranking for a keyword you target, not a feature or category peer: search each main keyword on both stores, keep the top 5 to 10 results, confirm they get downloads. Two more finders: cross-usage data and your own brand term in a keyword tool (who the algorithm clusters you with). Paid set (dated: 2024): your app, 2 or 3 direct competitors, every repeat top advertiser on your terms.
- Record each competitor's categories. Apple recommends researching how similar apps are categorized before choosing your primary category. The primary category sets browse, filter and tab placement; both categories are indexed by search; an irrelevant category is grounds for rejection (Apple-documented). Your categories also pick your benchmark peers.
- Gate by scale, then trend: 30-day downloads and revenue against the number you need, then 90, 180 and 365 days plus year over year; avoid a declining app's strategy and a declining category. Pre-launch, gate before any code (dated: 2020; Procedures, item 4). Thought experiment first (rule of thumb, dated: 2020): 100,000,000 potential users is a yes, 100,000 debatable, 1,000 a no.
- Rank the set by revenue per download (contested): the 2026 rule omits the 2020 caveat that it misreads subscription and consumable apps, where revenue lags the download. Read it as fighting capacity ($0.64 per download cannot outbid $6.01) and as your paid cost-per-install ceiling (the 2020 word-game set ran $1.50 to $7.50), nothing more. Apple's D35 proceeds per download fixes a 35-day window but covers only your app against peers (Apple-documented).
- Size is not a filter: mine keywords from the top five at any size; take page strategy from the download leader in your league; small winners beside giants mean room. Room-for-entry signals (dated: 2020): no new entrant means entrenched incumbents; more ad networks mean a costlier fight; a shared age group leaves another open; a poor good-to-bad rating ratio means beatable.
- Drop the noise: Apple's own apps (number two for their head term, leaving four contestable ranks; stated January 2025, not re-checked), category leaders returned for brand searches, advertisers on unrelated terms, off-type apps.
- Check the money (observed): "weather" at popularity 72 had a top 10 at 530K downloads and $5M in 30 days, not a niche to enter. Ranking apps with no revenue mean the phrase is not worth the listing; a long-time spender on a popularity-5 term means money is there.
- Head terms owned by a marketed brand are not ASO targets ("Bible" at popularity 73, competitiveness 94: zero chance from a subtitle); in brand-heavy categories benchmark against the top 3, not the top 10 (dated: 2019).

## Benchmark against peers in App Store Connect

- Peer group benchmarks are Apple's only comparison with other apps and never reveal an individual app (Apple-documented). Use them to judge your app against the category, not to size a named competitor.
- Peers are grouped by each category you selected, by business model (subscription when at least 50% of revenue comes from auto-renewable subscriptions) and by weekly download volume in three tiers (Apple-documented); since September 2026 the volume group is an optional comparison. Tier boundaries are undocumented.
- They show eight metrics covering conversion rate, retention, crash rate, proceeds per paying user, D35 download-to-paid conversion and D35 proceeds per download, as 25th, 50th and 75th percentiles in weekly intervals, with differential privacy, opted-in usage data and Apple's own apps possibly included (Apple-documented, as of 2026). One travel group's conversion percentiles were 1.19%, 2.91% and 6.78%, an illustration, not a norm.
- Conversion rate is total downloads over unique impressions on the Analytics page, but product page viewers in Apple's reporting overview; confirm the definition before comparing.
- Act on a weak benchmark (Apple recommends): low conversion, product page optimization, custom product pages, localization; low retention, in-app events and onboarding; low monetization, the acquisition channels with the best D35 proceeds, a trial when download-to-paid is low, territory pricing when proceeds per paying user trail. Steps in Procedures, item 10.

## Read a listing

- The keyword field is never readable; everything about it is inference from rank patterns, and you say so. Name, subtitle, screenshots, ratings and update history are readable per localization; check every localization (observed), since a localized name alone can outrank more ratings.
- Explain every rank by term position (name, subtitle, inferred keyword field) and 30-day new ratings in that storefront (rule of thumb). Apple confirms the inputs, text relevance (title, subtitle, keywords, primary category) and user behavior (downloads, ratings and reviews, "and more"), but no weights, placement or window (Apple-documented). Open: a term held only in the subtitle, late in the name, or with few new ratings; a weaker app wins by placing it earlier (dated: 2022), best where most top results lack the full, unsplit term in their name.
- Four-combination test (dated: 2022): their metadata mistakes plus fewer new ratings than you, you rank once your listing is clean; no mistakes, fewer ratings, you likely rank with correct targeting; mistakes, more ratings, possible because the algorithm checks keyword use before performance; no mistakes, more ratings, pick another keyword. Parity is within about 20% of the top results' ratings (rule of thumb, dated: 2020).
- Four misreadings: a month-old app at number seven with one rating sits on popularity-5 terms and will fade; a brand ranking without the term in its name goes to number one the day it adds it; misspellings are normalized ("fltr" topped "filter", dated: 2022); special characters such as & and : truncated a trailing keyword (dated: 2022, not re-verified), so rule that out before reading a failure as an opening.
- Diluted names are openings (dated: 2019): an incumbent whose name mixes themes ranks high for none.
- Metadata history: track name and subtitle change dates per localization and store; frequent changes mean a fight, none an opening (rule of thumb). The timeline shows only what they tried; what worked takes the watch loop (Procedures, item 1).
- Screenshots: compare with the top one or two apps for your keyword, borrow only after checking their downloads, never copy an ad-heavy competitor's ad-tuned message; since June 2025 their captions are a keyword reference (Procedures, item 8). Rules in `references/screenshots-and-product-page.md`.
- Paid footprint: competitor Apple Ads data comes from daily scans of observed placements, so absence means low bids, not no campaign; a keyword they keep bidding on works. Per advertiser read impression share, organic rank and tenure: a new bidder is testing, a months-long one is executing (2026). An advertiser on your brand term intercepts people already looking for you. Rules in `references/apple-ads.md`.

## Keyword gap analysis

- Core method: lay the top five results' organic keyword lists side by side; look for terms one app ranks for and the others do not, or that nobody actively optimizes; a cluster the leader is not hyper-targeting marks a feature to highlight or build. Organic ranks only. Three opportunity types (dated: 2020): near-misses where you rank 6 to 15, repeatable patterns, low-competition long tail they use and you do not.
- Filter: medium popularity with at least one competitor at rank 1 to 10; drop the 5s; sort popularity ascending; keep terms where your ratings compare or name placement can beat them. Bands quoted before October 2025, including the older 10-to-100 band (dated: 2020), are too high on the re-normalized scale.
- Reach gate, two versions (contested). Dated 2019-2021: at least the number 5 result's downloads, ideally the number 3's. From 2022: 30-day new ratings and name placement, relative to whoever holds the term. Apple lists downloads beside ratings and reviews, so "ratings, not downloads" can only be an undocumented claim about weight. Run the ratings test; use downloads as a sanity check where estimates exist.
- A 1-to-100 ranking-difficulty score (keyword density, category rank, ratings, downloads and age of the top 10) only triages; decide on the values behind it (dated: 2019-2020). "Bible" at competitiveness 94 is that score.
- Work the stem: pool the top ten results' keywords, filter by root word, take the lowest-popularity variant that makes sense and that the top results lack in their names; iterate one level down from each candidate (dated: 2019-2020). Niche terms only some competitors use fall to an app with more ratings almost instantly. Competitors' descriptions seed ideas (dated: 2019), though Apple's text-relevance list leaves the description out.
- Run it per country and store from the local winners; for a new country add local competitors, switch storefront, sort by popularity, translate where needed (dated: 2024). Secondary localizations add characters, not room (dated: 2020).
- Measure head-term depth first (observed): "habit tracker" shows 241 results but 1,300 apps carry it in the name; an "AI image generator" app with 130 ratings a month sat below position 80 against top results earning about 740. Ratings velocity is the number to beat (rule of thumb; Apple speaks of the number and quality of ratings, not velocity).
- Big-spender exact-match titles (contested): one view avoids any keyword a big spender holds exact-match in its title (Replika for "AI friend"); the other says number 2 or 3 for "AI friend" still wins downloads, unlike number 2 for "Replika".
- The same table feeds paid: top advertisers' paid keywords or similar apps' popularity 5 to 10 terms make a long-tail campaign (Procedures, item 6; `references/apple-ads.md`). After your own metadata change, benchmark a competitor over the same window (Procedures, item 2; `references/measurement-and-cadence.md`).

## Growth signals readable from outside

One method shown on one app (PicsArt, late 2025 to January 2026), lightly tested; confidence medium.

- Read download and revenue trends as a pair: falling downloads with rising revenue means hyper-targeting fewer, more primed users (observed: PicsArt at 6.9 million downloads and $8.2 million in 30 days, downloads negative year over year since January 2024, revenue rising).
- Split by country first: PicsArt's downloads were 31% India and 6% US. Localize where a competitor is weak, and where they localize you must too (2026); put ad spend where competitors do well (dated: 2020). See `references/localizations.md`.
- A collapse in a competitor's rating volume marks a change that drove users away; read the reviews around that date. Downloads per rating (30-day downloads over 30-day new ratings, dated: 2022) shows who prompts (observed): Cal AI at 15 to 17 against MyFitnessPal's 24 to 25. A competitor's figure is how many downloads you need to out-earn them on new ratings.
- Demographics set targeting, keywords and messaging; read them by age band, since the largest band's gender ratio can differ from the aggregate. They are model estimates (targeting rules in `references/meta-ads.md`). Cross-usage gives a targeting profile, an ad audience and a keyword source; many rivals in it means low loyalty.
- Under the hood (observed inferences): SDK detection is signature matching, can show SDKs later removed, and its accuracy is undiscussed. Attribution SDKs mean measured paid spend that will continue; engagement and in-app chat SDKs mean push- and support-driven retention; data-collection labels show instrumentation depth; a new permission with no matching feature is a roadmap leak (Procedures, item 5).
- Paid presence: start Apple Ads where competitors run it, which in 2026 is most of the top 100. Skip terms where one app holds most of the impression share (53% to 60%, or three apps at about 85%); harvest where it is spread (dated: 2024). Meta's Ad Library shows live ads. See `references/apple-ads.md` and `references/meta-ads.md`.

## Mine reviews and custom product pages

- Reviews are the pitch source; several competitors at once surface category language faster. Star bands differ by question (contested). What to say: one- and two-star (two- and three-star also work): positives are what to show, negatives what to reassure about, disbelief calls for social proof; a 2026 variant reads 1-star alone. What to build: the three-pass method (dated: 2020; Procedures, item 3). Recurring review words are also ad and screenshot angles. Apple's LLM-generated review summaries are a quick first pass where shown: the United States and 8 English-language storefronts as of November 2026, in a phased rollout (Apple-documented).
- The real competitive features are what users praise, often small and cheap. Specific pain points are actionable, generic "buggy" is not; one or two mentions make nothing important.
- Find what users of same-job competitors want and lack that you have, then put it in screenshots, description and, by its size, the keyword field, subtitle or name. Unhappy incumbent users (price, paywalled saving) are already looking for an alternative.
- Custom product pages show how a competitor positions itself and which segments it chases. A page is visible only through its link or in search for keywords assigned to it, where it replaces the default page (Apple-documented), so there is no public index; a market intelligence tool can discover pages, but ad-only pages may stay hidden. Pages tied to ad money are the tested segmentation and correlate with the keywords routed to them. 83 of the top 100 grossing apps had them in mid-2025; a big studio with none is an opening. Observed sets: Audible 13 and Scan Hero 35, both read against the old cap of 35; the limit is 70 per app (Apple-documented). Rules in `references/custom-product-pages-and-events.md`.

## From findings to positioning

- Never copy wholesale: assemble the strategy from what several apps do well, reuse the method, never the keywords; Apple features what offers something new (dated: 2019). Copying a popular app or lightly changing another app's name, icon or UI violates guideline 4.1, and impersonation risks removal from the Apple Developer Program (Apple-documented).
- Never put a competitor's name in your metadata: competing app names in keywords are a common rejection reason, the subtitle must not reference other apps, and no other developer's brand or name may appear in your icon or name (Apple-documented). Screenshots must not reference other apps with intent to mislead (Apple-documented); factual mentions are unaddressed, so "import from TikTok" is fine and "just like TikTok" is not. Comparisons also fail commercially: a brand searcher downloads the brand (Paprika).
- Competitor bidding is (contested): skip competitor names unless you have money to burn, and defend your brand only where it became the generic term (dated: 2021); Apple Ads Help recommends a competitor campaign among four starter campaigns, aimed at searches for similar apps in the same or a related category, exact match (Apple recommends). Apple does not say whether that means brand names.
- Beat competitors on the one or two keywords they handle badly (video dating or video chat, not both plus cam and flirt, beside Tinder); chasing the biggest keywords is a repeated founder mistake, and Apple recommends weighing less common terms against highly competitive ones, especially for newer or smaller apps. Put the opportunity keyword first in the name (dated: 2022).
- Overtaking a leader is a distribution and rating-conversion play (observed): Cal AI outranks Lose It with 205K US ratings against 720K on cheap TikTok and Reels volume at $30 a year; Scan Hero nets half a million dollars a month in a commodity niche through heavy ads and 35 use-case pages.
- In copycat niches the fight is presentation: TV remote apps share names, palettes and icons, several earn hundreds of thousands, and "TV remote" at popularity 63 is winnable by ASO alone. Stay inside guideline 4.1: a unique name and your own icon. Expect lower page conversion there, because shoppers compare similar apps (dated: 2021; Apple benchmarks within categories, never across); an icon color unlike the niche is the counter (dated: 2019).
- Challenger recipe (PicsArt): match engagement and campaign intensity, target its strong countries, undercut price, take evenly shared paid terms and the long tail, build a page per under-served feature. Where incumbents run ASO and Apple Ads, plan for both; where nobody can name a search term, use Meta ads.

## Procedures

Popularity thresholds are on the pre-2025 scale unless marked.

1. Watch loop (dated: 2020): list competitors' ranked keywords next to yours; log name and subtitle changes with dates; correlate with rank movements; mine related keywords; check download estimates to confirm impact.
2. Benchmark after your own change (dated: 2020): record share of targeted keywords ranked, average rank and top-10 count on popular terms; ship; wait four weeks; record again; run the same on a competitor over the same window; compare deltas.
3. Three-pass review mining (dated: 2020): pass one, loved features in 4- and 5-star reviews via positive words (great, love, awesome, amazing); pass two, pain points in all stars via negative words (hate, terrible, bad, slow, load, missing); pass three, nice-to-haves in 3- and 4-star reviews via suggestive words (wish, add, recommend, suggest, need); refine each with the recurring theme word, tally, rank by count.
4. Pre-launch validation (dated: 2020): for the top results on the idea's main keyword, read size (30-day downloads and revenue), growth (18 to 24 months of downloads), willingness to pay (revenue per download as the cost-per-install ceiling), room (the four signals), their categories and the SDKs leaders ship.
5. Tech-stack review (dated: 2021): per competitor, active development from paid analytics, bug-reporting or performance SDKs; cross-platform speed from the framework; the most-installed ad SDKs as a trial shortlist, about 30 days each; re-engagement from engagement SDKs; support reach from CRM or live-chat SDKs.
6. Paid set and share of voice (dated: 2024): load the paid set; filter to overlapping keywords; sort by impression share one app at a time; avoid large gaps, raise bids on small ones; harvest their paid keywords over 90 days minus yours and the irrelevant; move those where you rank below 10 organically into metadata after the popularity and competition check.
7. Size-up, teardown, weekly brief (2026): size-up is last month's downloads, revenue and store presence; teardown adds six-month trends, category rank history, organic and paid keywords, 1-star themes, three exploitable weak spots; weekly, the top 5 competitors' 7-day changes in downloads, revenue, ratings and ranks, action items first, with your own alert threshold.
8. Caption audit (June 2025): open each top result's screenshots for a keyword; note caption text, position and relation to the app across direct, similar and adjacent apps; write one active caption per screenshot that reinforces a name or subtitle keyword.
9. Burst planning (dated: 2020; recorded, not endorsed, a games tactic): from the top 5 to 10 apps' rank and daily downloads, pick the weekday where the target rank needs the fewest downloads and no competitor bursts.
10. Peer benchmark check (Apple, 2023 and 2026): open Benchmarks in App Store Connect Analytics; confirm the peer group's category and business model; compare each metric with the percentiles; open View Trends for weak ones; switch volume group, category (primary, secondary or all), percentile and date range; act with Apple's map.

## What changed

- 2019: keyword-stuffed names stopped being rewarded. 2022: the algorithm model moved from "downloads and ratings" to "new ratings, not downloads", the root of the reach-gate conflict. Early 2023: Apple's peer benchmarks shipped (announced WWDC22).
- 2024: competitor paid-keyword datasets from daily App Store scans. April 15, 2025: Apple Search Ads became Apple Ads.
- June 9, 2025 (WWDC25, iOS 26): custom product pages reach organic search through assigned keywords (Apple-documented), so a competitor's pages reveal its organic intents. November 2025: the page limit rose from 35 to 70.
- June 2025: screenshot text indexed. 2025: D35 metrics joined the benchmarks. Fall 2025: popularity re-normalized; pre-October bands read competitor lists as emptier than they are.
- Late 2025 to 2026: the biggest new-app surge in about 9 years (March 2026 releases up 118% year over year). March 2026: second ad slot, so organic means top three. Summer 2026: every app, Tinder included, carries keywords in its name.
- September 2, 2026: eight benchmark metrics, volume group optional, Games app icon views counted as impressions.
- November 2026: market intelligence tools expose estimates, demographics, cross-usage and SDK data to AI agents; a teardown becomes a prompt, the weekly brief a schedule; the data stays estimates.

## Checks

- Competitor set: top 5 to 10 organic results per keyword, noise dropped, with 30-day and 90/180/365-day figures, revenue per download, room-for-entry signals and categories.
- Paid set: impression share, organic rank and tenure per shared keyword.
- Peer benchmarks: group confirmed, metrics placed against percentiles, weak ones mapped to Apple's action.
- Per competitor: metadata change dates per localization, 30-day new ratings per storefront, downloads per rating, custom product pages, live Meta ads, SDKs with removals.
- Gap table: top five's organic keywords side by side, 5s dropped, popularity ascending, with name placement, 30-day ratings and four-combination class; misreadings flagged.
- Review themes per competitor: show, reassure, prove; loved, pain, nice-to-have with counts.
- Positioning: no competitor in name, subtitle, keywords or icon; no misleading reference in screenshots; structure, not tokens, copied; opportunity keyword first; competitor bidding flagged as contested; estimates labeled.
- After any metadata change: your three metrics and a competitor's over the same four weeks.

## Open questions

- The whole-company read is a lightly tested method shown on one app; SDK detection accuracy is undiscussed, and no Apple number can validate an estimate.
- Reach gate: downloads (2019-2021) versus new ratings plus name placement (2022 on), never compared; Apple lists both inputs without weights.
- Revenue per download misreads subscription apps, with no correction; Apple's D35 benchmark covers only your app.
- Whether Apple's competitor campaign means brand names is unstated; the 2021 advice against brand bidding is unreconciled.
- Big-spender exact-match titles: avoid, or accept rank 2 or 3. Metadata history shows what was tried, never what worked.
- Review star bands disagree, none tested against switchers.
- Pre-launch figures are unrefreshed 2020 estimates; burst planning is unconfirmed for non-games. 2019-2022 thresholds (popularity 10 to 100, parity within about 20%, below rank 10) are untested on the re-normalized scale.
- How a third party finds ad-only custom product pages is unknown. Benchmark tier boundaries are undocumented.
- Niche counts are name or description matches; whether vibe-coded apps displace incumbents rests on one example. Apple's own apps at number two (January 2025) is unverified since; the weekly brief has no alert thresholds.
